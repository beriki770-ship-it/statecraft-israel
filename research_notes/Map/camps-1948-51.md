# The named military and reception camps, 1948-51 (#1719, research part)

Status: **draft, needs a person to check**. This is research only. An AI agent opened every source marked "agent read" below
on 26 October 2026 and recorded the day it opened each. `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` section 2, rule 6, still requires a person
to check every claim against its source before use. This note does not change `content/`, `sim/` or `unity/`, and it does not
propose the starting reception host: it lists the facts the owner needs to pick it (section "Facts for the starting reception host").

## Question and result

#1719 proposes eight named camps: `military_tel_litvinsky`, `military_schneller`, `military_tzrifin`, `military_camp_80`,
`reception_atlit`, `reception_rosh_haayin`, `reception_pardes_hanna`, `reception_beit_lid`. For each: coordinates, 1948-51
operating dates, period names, and what it must not be confused with.

**What closed and what did not.** The only primary sources this box could open were the Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) daily
bulletins (`pdfs.jta.org`). Every Israeli primary archive was closed to this box on 26 October 2026 (see "What was tried and
failed"). So:

- **The verified facts** all come from JTA bulletin pages read as images: the capture of Sarafand (Tzrifin) on 19 May 1948; Atlit
  as a prisoner-of-war camp on 14 November 1948; Beit Lid as a reception camp in October 1949, February 1950, May 1950 and
  (as a film location) July 1951; and a Rosh HaAyin Yemenite immigrant camp on 17 May 1950.
- **Every coordinate is [S]** (Hebrew Wikipedia or a Wikipedia-fed point). No British Mandate map scan could be opened and
  georeferenced here, so no coordinate is verified.
- **Opening days stay [GAP]** for Atlit (as an immigrant camp), Rosh HaAyin, Pardes Hanna and Beit Lid. Hebrew Wikipedia gives a
  month or a year for some of them; it cites press this box could not open.
- **No source read gives a day the military camps stopped being military**, and none was expected to: Schneller, Tel
  HaShomer and Tzrifin stay military bases to this day (Hebrew Wikipedia, [S]).
