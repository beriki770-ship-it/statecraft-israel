## Question and result

Refs #1871. The issue proposes al-Malikiyya from 6 June 1948 as the anchor of the Lebanese army's map-only token (Dupuy p. 47;
Morris p. 260 still to be checked) and asks for a named frontier place for 15 May 1948, and whether and when al-Malikiyya fell
in Operation Hiram. The row under discussion is `lebanese_army` in `content/map/units_1948.yaml`.

**Short answer.**

- **6 June at al-Malikiyya: Dupuy p. 47 says it.** Dupuy has the combined Lebanese, Arab Liberation Army (ALA) and Syrian attack
  on 6 June, with the Lebanese capturing the town "for the second time" by evening. Morris p. 260 could not be opened [GAP]. The
  JTA reports the loss of the place on 7 June (dateline 8 June) and credits "Arab units" in a Syrian context, not Lebanese: [C].
- **15 May: no source read here names a place that the Lebanese army held.** Dupuy p. 45 has the Lebanese taking al-Malikiyya
  after a dawn counter-attack on 15 May (this is the account the owner's ruling on #226 set aside in favour of the ALA at
  al-Malikiyya). The JTA bulletin of 17 May says only that the Lebanese "penetrated the Israel border for a distance of
  approximately one mile" on 15 May, with no place. So al-Malikiyya on 15 May rests on Dupuy alone, in conflict with the
  owner's ruling [C]; the only place-neutral fact is the frontier near it.
- **Operation Hiram: al-Malikiyya fell to the Israeli 7th Brigade after Sasa and Saliha.** Dupuy p. 104 gives the order but no
  calendar day inside the operation (launched at dusk on 28 October, p. 102 per the text read; the Sasa phase ended on or after
  30 October). No JTA bulletin of 27 October to 8 November names the village.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Who held al-Malikiyya on 15 May.** Dupuy p. 45: the Lebanese, after a counter-attack from across the border. The owner's ruling
   on #226 (ruling 2; dated 11 October 2026 in `research_notes/GDD phase 5/arab-forces-1948.md`, section 5) puts the ALA there, from he.wiki and the official Lebanese account, which this box has not
   read. The JTA of 17 May has the Lebanese a mile inside the border with no place. Nothing read here settles it.
2. **6 June or 7 June for the second fall.** Dupuy p. 47: the Lebanese took it on the evening of 6 June. JTA 9 June (dateline
   8 June): Jews "ousted" on 7 June, by Arab units in a Syrian context. Both kept; the difference is one day and one attribution.
3. **Dupuy's Lebanese 15 May account against its own 28-29 May account.** Dupuy p. 46 has the Lebanese "motionless for a week"
   and Malkiya retaken by Yiftach on 28-29 May, so on Dupuy's own reading the village changed hands in this order: 15 May
   Lebanese, 28-29 May Israeli, 6 June Lebanese, Hiram Israeli.

## What the issue's question needs, as facts

- **A frontier place on 15 May that no source read here contradicts:** the border at al-Malikiyya and Kadesh (Dupuy p. 45),
  or "about one mile inside the border" on the northern front with no place (JTA 17 May). Dupuy's plan line (p. 43) names Nakura
  (an-Naqoura) to Nahariya as the coastal axis, as a plan, not a position.
- **For a map-only token from 6 June:** Dupuy p. 47 (second capture on 6 June), JTA 9 June (7 June, Syrian context).
- **Hiram:** Israeli capture after Sasa and Saliha, after 30 October on Dupuy's order of events; the exact day is not read.
