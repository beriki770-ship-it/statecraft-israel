# Sourced map places for the well, sandbags, crates and wall props (#1873, research part)

Status: **draft, needs a person to check**. This is research only. An AI agent opened every source marked "agent read" below
on 28 October 2026 and recorded the day it opened each. `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` section 2, rule 6, still requires a person

## Question and result

Refs #1873. Four props by Tal Heuberger (`docs/generated-buildings-props.md`, rows 09 to 12: "stone village well", "low sandbag
position", "supply crate stack", "low stone wall module") have no sourced map role. The issue asks, for each, for real 1948-51
places where such a feature is documented, with dates.

**Short answer.**

- **Sandbags: four dated places, all in Jerusalem, all 1948.** The walled Old City (report dated 16 January), the road outside
  Sheikh Jarrah (13 April), the British "Alamein camp" (26 April) and the Red Cross zone in the south of the city (5 to 6
  September). Each is a JTA dispatch, read on the page image.
- **Wells: five dated mentions, all 1950-51, none a village well of 1948.** Wells south of Nirim (dispatch of 4 April 1950),
  an "old Arab well" near the boring that supplied Eilat (event 31 July 1950), a well near Latrun (a Jordanian complaint,
  reported 28 March 1951), an "Arabs' well" on the Jordan side near a kibbutz printed as "Batar" (3 April 1951), and wells north
  of Beersheba feeding the pumping station of 20 June 1951. None is a 1948 village well, and none gives a position finer than the place named.
- **Walls: three dated mentions.** A "concrete defense wall" at the Jewish Quarter of the Old City (report dated 4 January 1948), "stone walls"
  beside the Sheikh Jarrah ambush (13 April 1948), and the "southern and northern walls of the Old City" at Mount Zion and the New
  Gate (28 September 1948). Only the second is a low stone wall in the sense of the prop; the first is concrete and the third is
  the city wall.
- **Crates: no place found where a stack of crates is documented.** The one mention that puts crates at a named place is a
  disputed account of the Jaffa bombing of 4 January 1948 (a vehicle "apparently loaded with orange crates"). A supply dump, a
  port shed or a kibbutz store with crates is [GAP].
- No source read here gives coordinates, a position inside a place, or the size of any of these features.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **The Jaffa vehicle of 4 January 1948.** The same JTA dispatch (5 Jan 1948, pp. 1 and 2) says the Arabs and some British
   insist it was a Jeep "driven up by Jews disguised as British soldiers", while other British authorities assert it was a
   three-ton truck "apparently loaded with orange crates and driven" by men in Arab dress (the line is partly cut, the rest
   of the words are read from the page). The crates are part of the disputed account, and the dispatch dates the explosion,
   not the crates [C].
2. **The Latrun well.** The only account is the Arab press in Jordan as relayed by JTA; the bulletin gives no Israeli answer
   and no day for the complaint. It is recorded as a claim, not as a fact about the well [C, one side only].
